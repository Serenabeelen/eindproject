What is hot ice

Hot ice is sodiumacetate trihydrate, a chemical compound. When it's in a supersaturated solution with water it produces heat and salt crystals resembling ice. They are used in special hotpacks that are sold in store when it get's cold. The hotpacks are supplied with a metal shaped object that can initiate crystallization. They can be reused by boiling the heatpacks until all the crystals are dissolved.

Scientific explanation

Baking soda reacts with the vinegar creating a supersaturated solution of sodium acetate. When the solution cools down and comes in contact with the sodiumacetate crystals crystallization initializes. That proces forms a lot of heat and it creates the appearance of ice. Thus the name of hot ice.

Instructions

1. Add the bakingsoda in a pan and slowly add vinegar. The vinegar reacts instantly with the bakingsoda creating fizz. Add more vinegar if there is bakingsoda visible in the pan.
2. Let the solution boil for 30 minutes or more. About 90% of the water should be evaporized.
3. Scrape the sodiumacetate crystals with a spoon from the side of the pan and collect them in a small bowl.
4. Put the remaining liquid inside a closed container with 1 or 2 tabelspoons of vinegar.
5. Cool the liquid inside an ice bath for 15 minutes.
6. Take the closed container with liquid and add some sodiumacetatecrystals. Hot ice will form. Be careful not to burn yourself. Sodiumacetate is foodsafe.
